# ADR 0048 — An Angular bridge, and Angular installed to build and test it

- Date: 2026-11-14
- Status: accepted (2026-11-15), with what a review of that day adds to the decision: the licences, the Node the workspace then needs, and the version the gate tests; the bridge is written and judged outside the gate until the packages are installed

## Context

Angular is the priority target (ARCHITECTURE.md §7.12): the rules of `@yaltaan/rules` exposed as validators of Angular Signal Forms, the nodes of `@yaltaan/form` as signals, no state of the bridge's own, Angular 22 and later (ADR 0006). The ticket is accepted once the pilot runs in a real Angular application, under component tests, within the size budget of the adapter (`yaltaan/angular-adapter`, 1,500 B brotli, a hypothesis).

Facts read on 2026-11-14 in the declarations of `@angular/forms` 22.2.4:

- `@angular/forms/signals` exports `form`, `validate`, `validateTree`, `validateAsync`, `validateStandardSchema`, `required`, `disabled`, `hidden`, `readonly`, `apply`, `applyEach`, `applyWhen` and `submit`, among others. The state of each field holds signals: `value`, `errors`, `valid`, `invalid`, `pending`, `dirty`, `touched`, `required`, `disabled`, `hidden` and `readonly`.
- Without a bridge, an application already reads a schema of `@yaltaan/schema` through `validateStandardSchema`, and rules through `refine` of `@yaltaan/rules` (`tests/interop/angular.mjs`, since T2.2 and T2.3). What it writes by hand is what a rule says field by field: `form-rules.mjs` writes one Angular validator per rule, which reads its other fields through `valueOf`, and one `required(…, { when })` per field a rule may require.
- The recipe `fromNode` of the reactive guide turns a node of the graph into an Angular signal (`tests/interop/reactive-angular.mjs`).
- The form shows `disabled`, `hidden` and `readonly` as they are, and `dirty` on a read-only field where Angular hides it (ADR 0047, `tests/interop/form-conditions.mjs`): a bridge that copied Angular's states would change what the form means.
- Nothing in the workspace installs Angular. `tests/interop/run.sh` installs it into a temporary project, outside the gate. A package that imports `@angular/core` cannot be compiled by the gate without it, nor its component tests run.

## Options

1. **Native: no bridge.** The application keeps `validateStandardSchema`, `refine` and the recipe, and writes its `required` and the placement of each rule by hand, field by field, as `form-rules.mjs` does; the pilot is never run in Angular. Costs the glue each application repeats, and leaves the ticket's criterion unmet.
2. **A bridge, with Angular installed as a development dependency of the workspace**, the bridge compiled against Angular's own declarations and its component tests in the gate. Costs the installation measured below.
3. **A bridge compiled against declarations of our own** of the few Angular types it uses, tested only outside the gate, by `tests/interop/`. Costs a copy of Angular's types that drifts silently from Angular's, and a gate that judges nothing of the bridge: a rule the gate does not check is not applied.
4. **A bridge in a repository of its own.** Rejected: it moves with the contracts of `@yaltaan/form` until they freeze (T2.7), and the graph of ARCHITECTURE.md §6 places it in this workspace.

## Measurements

On 2026-11-14, Ubuntu 26.04 x64, npm 11.19.0, each set installed into an empty project with `--ignore-scripts` and exact versions:

- **Angular**, to compile the bridge and run it under Node without a DOM (`@angular/common`, `@angular/compiler`, `@angular/core`, `@angular/forms` and `@angular/platform-browser` 22.2.4, `rxjs` 7.8.2): 9 packages, `tslib`, `zod` and `@standard-schema/spec` brought by `@angular/forms`; 58 MB; MIT 7, Apache-2.0 1 (`rxjs`), 0BSD 1 (`tslib`); no install script.
- **Angular and jsdom 30.1.3**, the DOM in which `tests/interop/` already runs Angular's `TestBed`, to render components: 45 packages, 84 MB; two of them under MIT-0 (`@csstools/color-helpers`, `@csstools/css-syntax-patches-for-csstree`), MIT without its attribution clause, a licence outside the list of CONTRIBUTING.md; no install script.
- **Angular and happy-dom 21.0.4** instead of jsdom: 18 packages, 81 MB (a second `@types/node` among them), licences within the list; not tried under `TestBed`.
- **The workspace today**: 47 third-party entries in the lockfile, `node_modules` 109 MB without its caches. Angular adds 9 entries and 58 MB (about half again), with jsdom 45 entries and 84 MB.
- **The bridge**, written on 2026-11-15 in `tests/interop/angular-bridge.ts` and measured by `tests/interop/angular-size.ts` as `scripts/size-report.ts` measures a scenario: alone, Angular and the packages of the workspace left out, 3,120 B minified and 1,393 B brotli under esbuild, 3,188 B and 1,404 B under Rollup, within the hypothesis of 1,500 B; with what it brings of the packages (`checkRules`), 1,512 B and 1,524 B brotli.
- **Its judges**, run by `tests/interop/run.sh` (about a minute in all, the earlier scripts included): it compiles under TypeScript 7 and 6 against Angular's declarations with the options of the workspace; `angular-bridge.mjs` compares it, on 200 generated sequences of writes per family, with `@yaltaan/form` (every state through `signalsOf`; the errors and `required` of every field through `validateRules`, the rules at the root, on each item of a list, on a group and over keyed elements reordered) and counts what each write checks again; on generated models whose values on the way are absent, null or primitives, then objects again, with `checkRules` and `requiredPaths`, each error where the values alone say it lands; `angular-pilot.mjs` renders the pilot in two OnPush components without zone.js, for its 46 scenarios: 706 errors, 122 of them from the rules and 15 on keyed elements, and 208 required fields, each where the schema, `checkRules` and `requiredPaths` say. Twenty-nine mutations of the bridge written by hand, the reviewer's among them, all turn the judges red.
- **The review** (a second reader, given the question and the code, 2026-11-15) accepted the `REQUIRED` metadata, accepted with reservations one metadata per rule, `resolveKey` and `signalsOf`, and refused the reads through the field tree as first written: Angular builds no field under a value that was absent or no object, nor, once it becomes one, while no logic stands below it and no field there is asked for, so that a rule reading under it stayed stale; a declared `length` of a list crashed; `fromNode` subscribed before an injector already destroyed refused the end of the subscription. All three were fixed, his reproductions pass, and his cases joined the judges. On a second pass he refused nothing: he found a key absent from the model making its rule checked again on every write, the read then depending on the whole object above it, and a descent through inherited names (`constructor`) that no judge saw; both were fixed and judged. His last verdicts: the `REQUIRED` metadata accepted; one metadata per rule, the reads, `resolveKey` and `signalsOf` accepted with reservations: Angular's tree errors cost fields × errors on each change, the cap is per rule (two rules past it give 2,002 errors where `checkRules` gives 1,001), the cost of `resolveKey` and the views made in a `computed` over `items` are only written down. He measured, per write, by rows of a list (500, 1,000, 2,000, 4,000): 25.7, 73.6, 129.2 and 254.7 ms with every row in error, 9.5, 21.2, 37.8 and 73.0 ms with none, linear, Angular filtering the tree errors of the root for every field below it (T2.10).
- **Signal Forms**, read in its sources: every logic function of one kind at one path runs in one computation (`validateTree` included), so that a rule computed there is checked again whenever any rule beside it is; `valueOf` throws when a value on the way is absent (NG01901); no field exists for a key whose value is `undefined`, nor under a value that becomes an object while no logic stands below it, until a field there is asked for; `metadata(field, REQUIRED, …)` sets `required()` and the native `required` attribute without an error of Angular's.

## Decision

Option 2, with jsdom, and MIT-0 added to the list of licences.

- **Development dependencies**, exact versions, recorded at the root as the other tools are: the six packages above and jsdom. The lockfile check lists the licences it has met so far, MIT, Apache-2.0 and ISC: it gains the six this tree brings, BSD-2-Clause, BSD-3-Clause, 0BSD, BlueOak-1.0.0 and CC0-1.0, which the policy of CONTRIBUTING.md already admits, and MIT-0, which it gains: MIT without the duty to keep the notice, on two packages of jsdom's tree that tests alone load and nothing ships. Angular 22.2.4 asks for Node `^22.22.3 || ^24.15.0 || >=26.0.0`, and the workspace refuses an engine that does not match: the Node of development rises from 22.18 to 22.22.3; the Node the packages support does not move (ADR 0006). The gate tests the version installed, the latest 22.x at least 7 days old on the day of the install, and jsdom likewise; `^22.0.0` is then a promise the interoperability matrix has to hold (T5.7).
- **`@yaltaan/angular`**: depends on `@yaltaan/core`, `@yaltaan/rules` and `@yaltaan/form`; `@angular/core` and `@angular/forms` are peer dependencies, `^22.0.0`, a later major added once tested (ADR 0006).
- **API**, `@experimental` until the contracts freeze (T2.7):
  - `fromNode(node, options?)`: a `Signal` that follows a node of the graph, created in an injection context or given an `injector`, subscribed until that injector is destroyed, throwing what the node throws: the recipe of the reactive guide, made a function.
  - `signalsOf(node, options?)`: for a node of `@yaltaan/form`, each of its states as a signal made by `fromNode` on its first read (`raw`, `parsed`, `issues`, `required`, `dirty`, `touched`, `pending`, `submitted`, `disabled`, `hidden`, `readonly`), shown as the form gives them, `dirty` on a read-only node included; a group's `children` reached the same way; an array's `items` a signal of its item nodes, each row calling `signalsOf` on its own. The injector is captured at the call, so that a template may read a state first. Nothing is stored but the signals.
  - `validateRules(path, rules, options?)`: inside the schema function of Signal Forms' `form`, on the value at `path`, each rule checked in a metadata of its own, which reads only the paths the rule declares, each down to its first index or `anyElement`, the list then read whole, each from the deepest field on its way: past it, under an object, absent, a key that appears seen through the children Angular builds, under a list, `null` or a primitive, its value read and then its own keys (the length of a list), so that Angular checks a rule again only once one of those values changes; its issues placed in a second metadata, so that a field built or removed on the way of an issue places it again without checking; one `validateTree` gathers the errors, each issue but warnings an error whose kind is its code and whose message is `format(issue)` (the code by default, as `refine`), on the field its path names or the deepest one on the way, up to 1,000 per rule; a `{ key }` resolved by `resolveKey(segment, parent, values)`, as in `refine`, the error otherwise stopping at the list; an index resolved in what Angular holds, where the form resolves it in what the rule read. The fields that `required` of a rule names are marked through the `REQUIRED` metadata, which `required()` and the native attribute read, and not through `required(…, { when })`: Angular's validator would add an error of its own, by its own notion of empty (`false` among them), beside the rule's issue, which already says what is missing. Only a field a target of the rule names is marked, `anyElement` or an index standing for each item: Angular marks a field it is told of when the schema is defined, so that a field a rule requires under a target, not at one, is not marked (the rules of `@yaltaan/rules` require their targets).
  - Not in the bridge: markup or a directive (adapters impose no markup), Reactive Typed Forms (ADR 0006: when a pilot needs it).
- **Tests**: the bridge under Node in the gate, an injector without a DOM; the pilot rendered by components in jsdom under `TestBed`, in the gate; `tests/interop/` keeps comparing with Angular's own behaviour.
- **Until the install**: the bridge is written in `tests/interop/angular-bridge.ts`, beside the scripts that run the other libraries, and run by `tests/interop/run.sh`; the root `tsconfig.json` leaves it out, and nothing that needs Angular installed enters the gate or the build. Not in `packages/angular/`: the gate refuses a folder of `packages/` without a manifest, and a manifest would make it a workspace package whose peer dependencies npm installs. The file then moves to `packages/angular/src/index.ts` unchanged, and its judges become the package's tests.

## Consequences

- The workspace installs about half as much again, or nearly twice as much with jsdom; `npm ci` in the CI takes longer, and the audit reads more packages.
- Every component test runs in jsdom, which is not a browser: layout and focus are not judged there; the pilot's display is judged on what the components render.
- The rules read what Angular's model holds, as Angular's own validators do: a hidden or disabled field's value, and an empty sub-form as an object, where the form leaves them out of what it submits (ADR 0047); Angular shows no error on a hidden or disabled field. An application that wants the form's meaning in Angular applies a sub-form's schema and rules only while its box says so.
- `resolveKey` is called for each `{ key }` of each issue and required path, some thousands of times a check at most: a search through the list makes the check quadratic, a map of the keys built once per list does not; the documentation says so, and the pilot's tests use the search on lists of a few elements.
- The audit of the gate reads 44 more packages, at its lowest level, and a fix younger than 7 days cannot be taken: an advisory on jsdom's tree (`undici`, `tough-cookie`) that no test of ours can reach turns the gate red. jsdom is built there without `runScripts` or `resources`, so that it runs no script and fetches nothing. The fallback, should that happen more than twice a quarter: Angular alone in the gate (9 packages, the bridge judged on an injector without a DOM), the component tests back in `tests/interop/`.
- The pilot rendered in Angular is compared with the schema and the rules read on what Angular's model holds, not with the issues its scenarios expect: Angular keeps an empty sub-form as an object. The faithful version, a sub-form's schema and rules applied only while its box says so, is T2.6's.

## Review condition

Angular's tooling moving to TypeScript 7; Signal Forms changing the signatures the bridge uses in a major; happy-dom proven under `TestBed` with fewer packages than jsdom and no open advisory; the audit turning the gate red on jsdom's tree more than twice a quarter; a pilot that needs Reactive Typed Forms.
